name: editorial-reduction
description: Reduces a website by removing, merging and simplifying until every element earns its place, and requires any addition to name what it displaces. Use when asked to simplify, declutter, trim, tighten or cut down a page, and when deciding whether a proposed section, card or chart should be built at all.
---

The most important skill. Its bias is **subtraction**.

The failure mode it exists to correct: every addition is individually
justified, and none is ever asked to justify itself *against the
others*. That is how a page reaches 13 sections and 50 cards without a
single bad decision being made.

## Order of operations

1. **Remove** — does this need to exist at all?
2. **Merge** — does something else already say this?
3. **Simplify** — can it be prose, a row, or a disclosure?
4. **Add** — only if 1–3 genuinely fail.

## The mandatory question

> **What gets removed to make room?**

Applied to every addition. **Naming nothing is a rejection.** If the
budget is 6 sections and 2 components, something must yield.

## Tests each element must pass

**Section** — what does this communicate that no other section does?
**Chart** — what question does it answer, can text answer it better,
does it reveal a pattern, does it change understanding? A chart that
restates numbers printed beside it is decoration.
**Card** — is this a headline fact, or supporting detail wearing a
headline's clothes? Most cards are prose.
**Row** — would a person who cares about this story want this row? If
not, disclose it.
**Number** — does another surface already state it? If so, do they use
the same assumptions?

## Disclosure resolves most conflicts

When someone explicitly wants all 100 rows and the design wants 6:
`<details>` keeps the data and removes the noise. **Prefer disclosure to
deletion when the content was explicitly requested.** Deleting something
a person asked for is a decision only they can make.

## Do not

- Protect work because it took effort.
- Delete something requested without flagging the conflict first.
- Strip personality along with clutter — dry humour, stated assumptions
  and honest caveats are signal, not noise.
- Confuse "fewer words" with "clearer". Sometimes the fix is one good
  sentence replacing four cards.

## Output

Before/after metrics for every dimension touched, and for each removal a
one-line reason. If a target is missed, say so and say why — a missed
target with a reason beats a hit target with a fudge.
